3. **Using AI For Scripts And Assets**, authored from Manual §06.1–06.3. Covers the one-asset-at-a-time rule, the modeling prompt verbatim, and the headline pattern.
4. **Fulfillment Intro**, authored from Ops Design Doc §3. Walks the architecture stage by stage, names the three CSM gates (intake quality, compliance review, launch review), and flags what is automated today versus still manual.
5. **Client Intake Form Walkthrough**, authored from `assets/tag-client-intake-form.pdf` and Manual §06. Walks all nine sections, shows where the branded form lives, and calls out which fields feed the AI script-modeling prompt so a thin intake is understood to produce a thin script.
6. **Compliance segment** added to Complete Tax Offer Structure, from Manual §09.6 and its Open Items: clients are licensed practitioners under Circular 230 and state board advertising rules, and no specific percentage or dollar tax-savings guarantee ships without the client's written sign-off. Its own clearly marked segment, not a footnote.
7. Updates to three existing lessons where the recorded video predates the current standard, added as lesson body text rather than re-recorded video: Wistia checklist (Manual §06.4), A2P full SOP sequence and its two silent-failure traps (A2P Setup SOP), REMINDERS exact templates and wait-step config (Manual §10.2).
8. Every lesson above is entered through the admin course editor or the import path from 12.4, and appears in the correct position in the CSM course.
9. No credential values are introduced into any authored lesson text by this story.
10. Gate passes: `ng build --configuration production && ng lint && ng test --watch=false`.

## Decision, 2026-08-25: the sign-off task is closed, not assigned

Sam's call. The authored lessons do not wait on a review pass by Austyn or
Walter before reps read them.

**This closes a task that would otherwise have stayed open forever.** The story
listed "review pass with the person who owns the standard" as its one unchecked
item, and Sam has said the content is acceptable as it stands. An open task
nobody intends to do is worse than no task: it makes the story permanently
incomplete and teaches readers that unchecked boxes are decorative.

**What is being accepted, counted from the code rather than the prose.**
`AUTHORED_CHANGES` in `lib/course/authored-lessons.ts` holds **11 entries: 5
inserts and 6 edits.** Five lessons authored net-new, six existing lessons
rewritten or extended. All written in Austyn's and Sam's voice from their own
documents, live in `tag_automation`, unread by the person who owns the standard.

**This story states four different counts and none of them is 11.** Its prose
variously says "Nine changes", "four new lessons", "five rewritten", "five
lessons" and "three existing"; a first draft of this note said nine, a second
said eight. The only number worth keeping as written is 17, which is the
resulting course size rather than a change count. **The array is authoritative;
the prose is not, and should be reconciled to it.**

**Nothing in the suite would ever have caught this.** `authored-lessons.test.ts`
iterates `for (const c of AUTHORED_CHANGES)` at :129 and :139, so the array can
grow or shrink without a single assertion failing. The one literal "nine" in the
file is an unrelated assertion about the intake form's nine sections at :99.
An off-by-three in a story about accepting unreviewed content, applied to a live
database, is the worst available place for a number nothing checks. That is a deliberate
acceptance, not an oversight, and it is recorded here so a future reader does not
"discover" it as a gap.

**Two content findings from the authoring session stand regardless** and are not
closed by this decision: the outline mis-assigns the A2P traps (they are section
08 pixel traps; A2P's real blockers are the chat widget on the VSL page and the
EIN nobody captures on the sales call), and Fulfillment Intro was written without
its cited source because `Ops Design Doc §3` is not in the repo.
