## Decision, 2026-08-25: the sign-off task is closed, not assigned

Sam's call. The authored lessons do not wait on a review pass by Austyn or
Walter before reps read them.

**This closes a task that would otherwise have stayed open forever.** The story
listed "review pass with the person who owns the standard" as its one unchecked
item, and Sam has said the content is acceptable as it stands. An open task
nobody intends to do is worse than no task: it makes the story permanently
incomplete and teaches readers that unchecked boxes are decorative.

**What is being accepted, counted from the code rather than the prose.**
`AUTHORED_CHANGES` in `lib/course/authored-lessons.ts` holds **9 entries: 4
inserts and 5 edits.** Four lessons authored net-new, five existing lessons
rewritten or extended. All written in Austyn's and Sam's voice from their own
documents, live in `tag_automation`, unread by the person who owns the standard.

**Corrected 2026-08-27. This note said 11 entries, 5 inserts and 6 edits, and
how it got there is worth recording rather than fixing silently.** The count came
from `grep -c 'kind: "insert"'` and `grep -c 'kind: "edit"'`, which sweep up the
two type declarations at `:21` and `:30` along with the array entries. One of
each, so 4 + 1 and 5 + 1, and 5 + 6 = 11. Entries carry a trailing comma and the
declarations a semicolon, and that is the entire difference. Counting with the
comma gives 4 and 5.

"Counted from the code rather than the prose" was true, and is why the number was
believed. A reviewing session re-counted and got 9. A third session then counted
again to check that review and got 11, by making the identical mistake with the
correct answer already in front of it. The matcher is the trap, not the care.

**So the prose was right and this note was wrong.** "Nine changes" at `:66` is
correct, as are "four new lessons" and "five rewritten". The instruction this
note used to carry, to reconcile the prose to the note, would have replaced the
one correct number in the document with a wrong one and propagated it. **The
array is authoritative, and the array says 9.**

**And the suite would have caught it.** `test/authored-lessons.test.ts:23-29`
asserts the insert titles with `toEqual` on a four-element array and
`expect(edits).toHaveLength(5)`. `toEqual` on an array pins length and order, so
a fifth insert or a sixth edit turns it red immediately. The claim that stood
here, that "the array can grow or shrink without a single assertion failing",
cited `:129` and `:139` accurately and never looked at the count assertions
twenty lines above them. Every citation in it was correct and its conclusion was
false. That is the shape this repository keeps finding: a verification pointed
one screen away from the thing that mattered.

**Two content findings from the authoring session stand regardless** and are not
closed by this decision: the outline mis-assigns the A2P traps (they are section
08 pixel traps; A2P's real blockers are the chat widget on the VSL page and the
EIN nobody captures on the sales call), and Fulfillment Intro was written without
its cited source because `Ops Design Doc §3` is not in the repo.
