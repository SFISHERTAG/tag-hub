# Story 13.6 — Escalations desk

**Epic:** 13 — Lifecycle handoff and churn prevention
**Status:** Draft
**Depends on:** 13.5 for the automatic-raise path. The manual paths do not depend on it.

## Story

As a client, I want to report a problem with my service from the Hub. As a CSM,
I want every raised issue in one queue with a severity and a clock on it, so
that nothing sits unanswered until the client leaves.

## Context

Half of this is already shipped. `lib/bug-reports.ts` writes to the Firestore
`bugReports` collection with a `submitted / in_review / resolved / closed`
lifecycle. `app/api/bug-reports/route.ts` serves it, and Angular has the full
feature module: routes, nav item, form, page, specs, from story 10.4.

That is the client-raised half without severity, an assignee, an SLA, or a
TAG-side working view. This story extends that record rather than building a
second one.

## Why not a GHL pipeline

Recorded because it was the first instinct and the reasons matter later.

- **Clients do not log into GHL.** They log into the Hub. A client-facing report
  form cannot be a pipeline stage without giving every client a GHL seat.
- **Some clients are not in our GHL agency at all.** The 2026-08-21 audit found
  Bullet Proof Tax under Adwizar, Make Taxes Fair on HubSpot, and CRM Voice and
  Agent CRM as separate agencies. A GHL-based desk structurally cannot serve
  them. This reason alone is decisive.
- **Opportunity semantics are wrong.** An opportunity carries `monetaryValue`
  and closes won or lost. A ticket is neither, and every report reading the
  pipeline would have to learn to exclude them.
- **Per-client pipelines drift.** The template makes 25-plus pipelines identical
  on day one and nothing keeps them that way.

## Why not Postgres

Also recorded, because "we moved off Firestore" is a reasonable thing to believe
from reading the docs and it is not true. Firestore is the primary system of
record, including sign-in. The one feature mid-migration is courses, and it is
stalled (story 11.6). Putting escalations in Postgres would add a third pattern
to a migration that has not finished its first, and would mean rebuilding a
working surface to change stores.

If cross-client escalation reporting is later needed for the CS rollup, add a
Postgres projection then, following the `clients` pattern: Firestore primary,
Postgres denormalized for reporting. Not before.

## Acceptance criteria

1. One record type serves both origins, with an `origin` field distinguishing
   client-raised from TAG-raised. Not two collections.
2. Severity, assignee, and the client it concerns are on the record. A
   client-raised report carries its location automatically rather than asking
   the client which account they are in.
3. A TAG-side queue view across all clients, filterable by severity, assignee,
   and status. The existing per-user view stays for the client.
4. Each escalation has an SLA clock on first response, distinct from the stage
   SLA in 13.5. Breach is visible in the queue and notifies the assignee and
   their manager.
9. Unresolved past its tier threshold, an escalation reassigns up the reporting
   line from the `csm` table: CSM to CSD to executive. Thresholds are
   admin-adjustable without a deploy, and the climb can be overridden manually in
   both directions.
5. An SLA breach from 13.5 raises an escalation automatically, with origin
   `system` and a link to the client and stage that triggered it.
6. The client sees status changes on their own report. A desk the client cannot
   see the state of produces a second report for the same issue.
7. Existing `bugReports` documents remain readable. New fields are additive, and
   absent severity reads as unset rather than as the lowest value.
8. Styling flows from M3 tokens. No raw hex in any component `.scss`.

## Decisions, 2026-08-22

**Escalations notify.** The assignee is told on raise; a breach alerts them and
their manager. Same answer as 13.5, deliberately.

**The three-tier CS rollup applies, and it climbs on its own clock.** An
escalation left unresolved past its threshold reassigns from CSM to CSD, then to
executive. **The thresholds are admin-adjustable**, which is what makes this
safe: the risk of auto-climb is exec-level noise from clients who are simply slow
to reply, and a fixed clock cannot be tuned away from that. An admin changes it
without a deploy. Reporting lines come from the existing `csm` table.

Build the climb so a manual override is possible in both directions. Someone will
need to hold an escalation at their own tier, or push one up early.

## Open question

  service complaints, or does the nav item get renamed? Cosmetic, but it changes
  what a client thinks the form is for, which changes what they write in it.
