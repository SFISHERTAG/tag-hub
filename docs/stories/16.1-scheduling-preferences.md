# Story 16.1 — Scheduling preferences

**Epic:** 16 — Account settings
**Status:** Draft — **blocked on 7.8**
**Depends on:** 7.8 (map uids to GHL users), 10.9 (the settings surface)

## Story

As someone who takes booked calls, I want to set my conflict calendar, choose
which calendar my appointments land on, and set my working hours, so that the
booking link stops offering times I cannot take.

## The blocker, first

**There is no mapping from an app user to a GHL user.** Every one of these three
settings is per-person and has to be written against a GHL user id, and this
codebase cannot resolve one.

The single exception proves it: `lib/dashboard/owner-calendar.ts:110` filters
appointments by `tenant.ownerGhlUserId` — a field stored on the *tenant*, so it
works for exactly one person per client and nobody else.
`lib/sources/metric-source.ts:63` names the problem outright: assigned user ids
are "GHL user ids from a different identity space".

Story 7.8 exists to close that and is Draft. Nothing here can be built first —
not partially, not for owners only. A settings screen that silently writes one
person's hours onto the tenant's owner is worse than no screen.

## What is actually possible, once 7.8 lands

Checked against the scopes in `lib/ghl/oauth.ts`, which are already granted.

| Setting | Feasible? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Which calendar appointments land on | **Yes** | `calendars.readonly` lists them; the choice is our own preference and needs no GHL write |
| Working hours | **Probably** | `calendars.write` is granted and covers a calendar's open hours and team-member availability. Confirm against the live API before committing to it, the way 5.10 does |
| Conflict calendar | **No, not in this app** | See below |

### The conflict calendar cannot be built here

Connecting a personal Google or Outlook calendar so GHL can read busy times is
an OAuth grant between that person and Google or Microsoft. GHL performs it in
its own UI. Our app cannot proxy it, and no GHL scope exposes it.

**Corrected twice on 2026-08-23.** The first version recommended deep-linking
into GHL. That was wrong for most of the people who need this, for a reason
already written down in story 13.6 — and Sam has since confirmed it as a
deliberate constraint rather than an accident:

> **Clients do not log into GHL.** They log into the Hub.
> **Some clients are not in our GHL agency at all.** The 2026-08-21 audit found
> Bullet Proof Tax under Adwizar, Make Taxes Fair on HubSpot, and CRM Voice and
> Agent CRM as separate agencies.

A deep-link requires a GHL seat. So it works for TAG staff and fails for exactly
the population that takes client-side booked calls — `client_closer` and
`client_setter` are real roles in `ROLES`, and those people have Hub logins and
no GHL login. For the clients on another agency entirely, there is no link to
send them to.

So the options split by who is being served:

| Who | Deep-link to GHL | Works? |
|---|---|---|
| TAG staff (CSM, closer, setter) | They hold a GHL seat | **Yes** |
| Client-side closers and setters | No GHL seat | **No** |
| Clients on another agency | Not our GHL at all | **No** |

Three real choices remain, and none is small:

**A. Deep-link, TAG staff only.** Ship it for the people it works for and say so
on the screen. Honest and cheap; leaves client-side callers with no conflict
detection, which is where double-bookings actually hurt.

**B. Our own Google Calendar OAuth.** `app/api/auth/google/route.ts:21` records
why sign-in asks for `openid/email/profile` only: non-sensitive, no Google
verification. Calendar scopes are sensitive, and requesting them puts the whole
application into a review on an external reviewer's schedule. This is the only
option that serves everybody, and it is a compliance project rather than a
settings task.

**C. Give client-side callers GHL seats.** Defeats the reason the Hub exists,
costs a seat per person, and does nothing for clients outside the agency.

**Decided 2026-08-23, by Sam: keep the client out of GHL.**

That is a product constraint, not a preference, and it settles the question by
elimination. C is out — giving a client a GHL seat is the thing the Hub exists
to avoid. A survives only as what it always was, a TAG-staff convenience, and
must not be presented to a client as their route to conflict detection.

**So for client-side callers it is B or nothing.** There is no third path: no
GHL scope exposes the connection, and the client will not be in GHL to make it.

**B is probably cheaper than the first version of this story implied**, and the
distinction matters enough to check before pricing it. Google grades OAuth
scopes as sensitive or restricted. Restricted scopes — Gmail message content,
for instance — require an annual third-party security assessment, which is the
expensive one. Calendar scopes are **sensitive**, which requires app
verification and brand review but not that assessment. Confirm against Google's
current policy rather than this paragraph, because the tiers move.

What still holds: verification is an external reviewer on their own schedule, it
applies to the whole OAuth client rather than one feature, and
`app/api/auth/google/route.ts:21` shows sign-in was deliberately scoped to stay
out of it. Entering it is a decision about the application, made once.

**Next step is not code.** Price B properly — read Google's current sensitive
scope requirements, confirm calendar is not restricted, and find out what
verification actually asks for. Then decide. Until then this story ships stage
1 and 2 only, and the conflict calendar stays out.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Blocked until 7.8 lands. Nothing in this story ships against
   `tenant.ownerGhlUserId`.
2. A scheduling section in `/settings`, visible only to users who resolve to a
   GHL user.
3. Default calendar: lists the tenant's calendars and stores the choice. The
   stored value is a calendar id plus the location it belongs to, so a tenant
   change cannot leave a dangling id pointing at another client's calendar.
4. Working hours: per-day ranges and a timezone, written through
   `calendars.write`. **Verify the endpoint against a live sub-account before
   building the UI**, per the rule 5.10 follows.
5. Conflict calendar: **not in scope until the A/B/C decision above is made.**
   If A is chosen, the screen shows the link only to users who hold a GHL seat,
   and tells everyone else plainly that conflict detection is unavailable for
   them — rather than showing a link that leads to a login they do not have.
6. A user with no resolvable GHL identity sees why, not an empty form.
7. All writes go through a typed service in `lib/ghl/`. No raw `HttpClient` in a
   component, per the architecture contract.
8. M3 tokens only, no raw hex, `OnPush`, standalone.

## Dev notes

**The timezone is not a detail.** Working hours without one are wrong for
everybody except whoever set them. GHL stores a timezone per calendar; decide
whether the user's hours follow the calendar's timezone or carry their own
before designing the form, because retrofitting it means reinterpreting every
saved value.

**Do not let this become the Google verification project by accident.** The
conflict calendar is the one item here that can quietly turn a settings story
into a compliance review. If someone proposes "just add the calendar scope",
that is the decision being made, and it should be made deliberately.
