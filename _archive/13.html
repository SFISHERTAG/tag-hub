Iteration

What to change, in what order, and what to leave alone.

StageChangeRationale
Iteration 1Open targeting, one creative, single calloutEstablish a baseline and let Meta find the audience
Iteration 2Split the callout into separate ad sets by segmentIsolate which audience the copy actually converts
Iteration 3Creative variation against the winning calloutOnly once the audience is known
DiagnosticDevice or connection filtersUse when lead quality is poor, as a test — not a default
The operating principle

We test copy, not targeting. The assumption is that Meta's delivery optimization outperforms manual audience construction, so the lever we pull is the callout. If a Client's ad names five different audiences at once, that's a signal to split it into a proper test rather than a finished ad.

§13.1 Worked example

An ad calling out executives, leaders, CEOs, and doctors simultaneously isn't one ad — it's four untested hypotheses in a trench coat. Iteration 2 for that account is four ad sets at $25/day, each naming one audience, running the same three creatives. Within a few days you know which callout carries the account, and iteration 3 puts full budget behind it.

§13.2 Efficiency notes

- Build a snapshot and deploy every funnel from it. - Clone, don't build. Every additional step should be a clone, stripped and edited. - Set mobile sizing before cloning. See 07.3. - Add the Client's logo to calendar settings. Quick, and visibly raises perceived quality. - Cap the booking window at 5–7 days. Longer windows produce cold, low-show bookings.
