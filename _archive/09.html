Audience Network re-toggles itself

After you switch it off, Meta frequently turns it back on when you change another setting. Turn it off, then check it again immediately before you publish. Same applies to multi-advertiser ads.

§09.3 Starting budgets

Constrained budget floor: $50/day — Workable with one creative, slower to signal Standard recommendation: $100/day — Reaches a decision point in 1–3 days Three or more creatives: $100–150/day — Bump so each creative gets data Aggressive first launch: $150–200/day — Where the Client's budget supports it Iteration 2 audience split: ~$25/day each — e.g. four callout variants at $25

Faster spend produces faster clarity. A Client at $100/day reaches a working configuration meaningfully sooner than one at $50/day — worth saying out loud when they're deciding.

§09.4 Ad copy structure

1. Callout — name the exact person. "Business owners earning $500K+ only." 2. Offer and guarantee — the outcome plus the risk reversal. 3. CTA — "Click the link below to book your strategy call."

The callout also does targeting work. Because detailed targeting is left open, the copy is the primary signal telling Meta who should see this ad. That's why we test the callout and leave the targeting alone.

Be specific, not generic

"High income attorneys" is weak. "Attorneys making over $400,000 a year" is the callout. The more specific the income or role qualifier in the copy, the tighter the audience Meta delivers to — vague callouts pull in browsers, specific ones pull in the Client's actual buyer.

§09.5 Creative handling

- Editor delivers assets. Clients request revisions directly in the editing tool rather than routing through you. - Default is one ad, modeled on a proven template. More only when the Client wants aggression and can fund it. - Set thumbnails manually. Auto-selected frames are frequently unflattering. No measured performance impact, but it affects how the Client perceives the work. - Publishing multiple ads may require publishing each one individually.

Guarantee language

Source material includes a guarantee promising to reduce tax liability by a specific large percentage or refund. That carries two risks. The first is Meta rejection, which is recoverable. The second is not: our Clients are licensed accountants and CPAs governed by IRS Circular 230, state board advertising rules, and AICPA conduct standards. An ad guaranteeing a specific tax outcome can create licensure exposure for the Client — a far worse failure than a rejected ad. Standard: no specific percentage or dollar tax-savings guarantee without written sign-off from the Client's licensed practitioner. Guarantee the process and the engagement, not the number.
